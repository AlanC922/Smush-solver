    Guide

    How to use the solver

    1. Turn on No hints (hardcore)

      In Smush, open the settings and switch on No hints (hardcore). The solver sometimes tells you to stop early. With this setting on, tapping “I'm done” doesn't count as a hint, so you keep the Unassisted bonus.

    2. Enter the board

      Type the nine letters with the gold letter first, like EAGHITVWY. If the gold letter ended up somewhere else, tap its tile and it moves to the center.

    3. Pick the spicy letter

      Tap the letter that has the chili on it in the game right now, then press Start solving.

    4. Play the word, then report the spice

      Enter the suggested word in Smush. The chili jumps to a new letter. Tap that letter here, or press its key, and the solver plans the next word from the new position.

    5. If Smush rejects a word, say so

      The solver uses a large Scrabble dictionary, and Smush's own word list is smaller. If the game won't take a word, tap Smush rejected it and you'll get another one.

    6. Stop when the solver says so

      When the card says Stop here, tap “I'm done” in Smush. The tally shows the bonuses the solver plays for. The game may add a few extras of its own, like streaks.

    Scoring at a glance

    The gold letter is free and goes in every word. The other eight letters have 5 uses each.

    Each word

    Scrabble letter values × a multiplier that starts at 1 and adds:

    Each use of the spicy letter+1
    Each letter the word uses up (smush)+1
    Pangram using all nine letters (+4 if it's your first word)+2

    End of game

    Clean plate, every letter used up+50
    Unassisted, no hints+25
    Heavy lifter, 6 words or fewer+25
    Word hoard, 13 words or more+25
    Perfect game, pangram first + clean plate + no hints×2
    ICE COLD, 5+ words that never used the spicy letter×5

    ICE COLD is usually the biggest prize, which is why the solver steers around the spicy letter and often stops early.

    Tip: Tapped the wrong spicy letter? Undo steps back one word. Your game is saved in this browser, so you can switch to Smush and come back.

    About

    How beam search solves Smush

    Smush is a planning game with luck in it. The letters you spend now decide which words you can play later, and the spicy letter jumps to a random tile after every word. The solver handles both by searching ahead through sampled futures. Every turn it runs four steps:

    1. Shortlist. Score every playable word by the final total you'd have if you stopped right after it, counting ICE COLD as if you'll keep it. Keep the best 12.
    2. Sample the luck. Draw 8 random paths for where the spicy letter jumps next. All 12 words face the same 8 paths, so the comparison is fair.
    3. Search to the end. For each word and path, run a beam search to the end of the game. It holds 24 partial games, lets each try its 8 best next words, merges games that used the same set of words, and keeps the best 24. At every step it records the final score for stopping there, bonuses included.
    4. Decide. Average each word's best result over the 8 paths. Suggest the top word, or say Stop if ending the game now scores more.

    That's 96 beam searches per turn, run in your browser. When you report the new spicy letter, the solver starts over from the real position.

    Why the bonuses matter

    Ranking lines by the final score with bonuses, not by word points alone, is what makes this beam+ instead of a plain beam search. Average final score over the same 100 random boards:

    StrategyAverage score
    Always play the highest-scoring word489
    Beam search for word points only718
    Beam+ with bonuses and early stopping (this site)1,018

    The browser version is a direct port of the original Python solver and returns the same values when given the same random draws.
